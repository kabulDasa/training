# Streetlifting: Transition Guide from Powerlifting

Streetlifting is weighted calisthenics as a strength sport — pull-ups, dips, and muscle-ups performed with a loaded dip belt, judged to strict competition standards. Think powerlifting's attempt structure (3 attempts, weight classes, judges) applied to bar work. Your deadlift strength transfers more than you'd expect.

---

## The Sport at a Glance

- **Federation:** ISF (International Streetlifting Federation) is the largest; WSF also active
- **Weigh-in:** 24–48 hours before competition (same weight-cut game as powerlifting)
- **Attempts:** 3 per lift, same strategy as powerlifting (conservative opener, realistic PR on 2nd, reach on 3rd)
- **Equipment:** Chalk allowed. Wrist wraps allowed. Belts allowed. **Straps banned.** Elbow/knee sleeves allowed.
- **Weight classes:** Structured similarly to powerlifting (u60, u67, u75, u90, etc. — varies by federation)

**Resources:**
- [Streetlifting: Everything You Need to Know (2025)](https://pursueperformance.com/streetlifting/) — clean overview of the sport, weight classes, and what to expect at a competition
- [Complete Guide to ISF Rules and Standards — Master Calisthenics](https://mastercalisthenics.com/articles/complete-guide-to-streetlifting-rules-and-standards/) — the rulebook equivalent; read before your first competition

## The 4 ISF Competition Lifts

### 1. Weighted Pull-Up

**Competition standard:**
- Start in a **dead hang** — arms fully extended, shoulders not shrugged, feet hanging or crossed
- Pull until **chin is clearly above the bar**
- No kipping, swinging, or half-reps
- Lower back to a full dead hang to complete the rep

**Technique guide:**
- Initiate by depressing and retracting the scapula (pull shoulder blades down and back first)
- Drive elbows down toward your hips — think "elbows to pockets"
- Keep core braced; slight hollow body prevents swing
- Grip: overhand (pronated) for competition standard; shoulder-width or slightly wider

**Chin-up → pronated pull-up transition (your specific situation):**
Chin-ups (supinated grip) are stronger for almost everyone — biceps co-contribute more. Switching to pronated will cost you 2–4 reps initially. The lat and scapular strength transfers directly; only the forearm position changes. Train both grips: chin-ups as your strength builder, pronated pull-ups as your competition standard. They'll converge within a few weeks.

**Common faults from powerlifting backgrounds:**
- Shrug-starting (using traps instead of lats to initiate)
- Not achieving a true dead hang at the bottom (powerlifters are used to tight, shorter ranges — full hang feels unfamiliar)

**Resources:**
- [The 4 Streetlifting Exercises Explained — Heavyweight Calisthenics](https://heavyweightcali.com/en/the-4-streetlifting-exercises-explained/) — covers all 4 lifts with competition standards; the bench press guide equivalent for streetlifting
- [Weighted Pull-Up Guide — MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/weighted-pull-ups-guide) — form breakdown with progressions

**Prerequisites before adding weight:** 8–10 strict dead-hang pull-ups

### 2. Weighted Dip

**Competition standard:**
- Start in a **full lockout** on parallel bars — arms fully extended, shoulders not shrugged
- Descend until **shoulders drop clearly below elbows**
- Press to a full lockout to complete the rep
- No swinging, bouncing, or partial range

**Technique guide:**
- Grip the bars firmly; keep torso slightly forward (not perfectly upright) for shoulder health
- Inhale on the way down, exhale on the press
- Scapula stays **protracted and depressed** throughout — this is the modern competition technique that reduces injury and ROM
- Control the descent; don't drop and bounce at the bottom
- Width: shoulder-width parallel bars are standard; adjust based on what your federation uses

**Depth note:** Shoulder below elbow is the minimum. Going excessively deep stresses the AC joint — reach the standard, don't overdo it.

**Common faults:**
- Forward lean collapse (chest drops excessively — leads to a pec-dominant, shoulder-stressing position)
- Incomplete lockout at the top

**Resources:**
- [Weighted Dips: The Ultimate Guide — Heavyweight Calisthenics](https://heavyweightcali.com/en/weighted-dips-2/) — the most comprehensive dip guide for competition context
- [Weighted Dips — MPDS Streetlifting](https://mpds-streetlifting.com/blogs/all-blogs/weighted-dips) — competition-specific technique details from an active federation

**Prerequisites before adding weight:** 12–15 strict bodyweight dips to competition depth

### 3. Weighted Muscle-Up

The most technical lift — a pull-up that transitions into a dip in one continuous movement.

**Competition standard:**
- Start in a **dead hang**
- Pull explosively; **transition over the bar** without kipping, swinging, or twisting
- Finish in a full lockout above the bar (same standard as top of a dip)
- Descent back to dead hang to complete

**Why this is a skill, not just strength:**
The transition (the "hip" or "turnover") is a learned motor pattern. Strong lifters regularly fail it because they haven't trained the false grip and the lean-forward at the top. Treat this separately from your strength training — it needs its own skill practice.

**The transition broken down:**
1. **Pull phase:** Explosive pull-up, aiming to get chest to bar (not just chin)
2. **Turnover:** At peak height, shoot elbows back and lean chest over the bar simultaneously
3. **Dip phase:** Press to lockout from the turnover position

**Prerequisites:**
- 10–15 strict pull-ups (explosive is better than slow and grinding)
- 5–8 explosive pull-ups where you can get chest to bar
- Comfortable with muscle-up negatives (start at top, lower yourself through the transition slowly)

**Progressions (in order):**
1. Explosive pull-ups to chest level
2. False grip hangs and pulls (false grip = wrist over the bar, thumb on the same side as fingers)
3. Muscle-up negatives (jump to top position, lower through transition under control)
4. Band-assisted muscle-ups (band reduces load at the hardest point)
5. Full muscle-up

**Resources:**
- [Muscle-Up Tutorial: Zero to Hero — Calisthenics Association](https://calisthenicsassociation.org/blog/muscle-up-tutorial-zero-to-hero) — the most structured progression guide; closest thing to Starting Strength's novice template for the muscle-up
- [How to Do a Muscle-Up — Eric Flag](https://ericflag.com/en/blogs/info/how-to-do-a-muscle-up) — detailed with video breakdowns; Eric Flag is one of the most cited coaches in competitive calisthenics
- [How to Do a Muscle-Up — Angles90](https://angles90.com/blogs/a90-blog/how-to-do-a-muscle-up) — covers both bar and ring muscle-ups; good for understanding the difference

### 4. Squat (ISF)

Same movement as powerlifting squat — some federations include it, others don't. If your target federation includes it, your powerlifting squat transfers directly. Confirm with the specific federation rulebook.

## The "Starting Strength" Equivalent for Streetlifting

**King of Weighted (KOW) by Micha Schulz** is the closest analog — a structured linear progression system for weighted pull-ups and dips, widely used as the entry point into competitive streetlifting. The concept mirrors Starting Strength: add weight every session while you can, wave load when you stall.

- [KOW Beginner Program — King of Weighted](https://kingofweighted.shop/products/beginner-program) — the standard beginner LP template for weighted calisthenics
- [Review: KOW Program — Straight Talking Fitness](https://straighttalkingfitness.com/2020/11/23/how-i-got-a-90kg-dip-a-60kg-pull-up-review-of-micha-schulzs-kow-strength-program/) — real-world results from someone who ran it (ended at +90 kg dip, +60 kg pull-up)
- [Streetlifting Program for Beginners & Intermediates — StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/streetlifting-program/) — free structured program with periodization; most beginner-friendly layout

**The core logic (applicable regardless of which program):**
- Train weighted pull-ups and dips on the same session, 3×/week
- Heavy day (~3–5 rep sets), medium day (~8 rep sets), light/volume day (10+ reps)
- Add 2.5 kg/week to each lift while you can (linear phase)
- When you stall: wave loads, add volume, or switch to a more advanced template

## Transferable Strength from Powerlifting

| Powerlifting Asset | Streetlifting Carryover |
|--------------------|------------------------|
| 210 kg deadlift — lat and scapular strength | Directly transfers to pull-up starting strength |
| Bench press — tricep and chest lockout | Transfers to weighted dip top-end |
| Competition experience — attempt strategy | Same format; same mental game |
| Belt and chalk familiarity | Both allowed in streetlifting |
| High training volume tolerance | Handles the frequency demand easily |

Your biggest edge: you already have the pulling muscle mass. You'll add weight to your pull-ups faster than someone starting from scratch.
