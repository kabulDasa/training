Swimming is technique-limited, not fitness-limited: move on when a stage feels calm, not when it feels hard.

## Stages

| Stage | Focus | Drills (per session) | Move on when |
|-------|-------|----------------------|--------------|
| 1 | Breathing | 10× bobs (sink, hum out through the nose, come up, breathe in); 5× face-down float, exhaling slowly | You exhale underwater without rushing |
| 2 | Kick + head turn | 4× 25 m kick on the front with a board; every 3–4 kicks turn the head to the side to breathe (one ear stays in the water) | 25 m without stopping or lifting the head forward |
| 3 | Body line | 4× 25 m side-kick: one arm forward, lie on your side, face down, roll to breathe | Steady on your side without sinking legs |
| 4 | Arms | 4× 25 m catch-up freestyle (one arm waits in front until the other arrives) | Breathe every 2–3 strokes without stopping |
| 5 | Freestyle | 4–8× 25 m freestyle, 30–60 s rest | 25 m without stopping |
| 6 | Distance | 50 m sets, then alternate freestyle/backstroke lengths: 4×50 → 2×100 → 200 | 200 m without stopping |

**Backstroke as the bridge:** you can already backstroke. Use it to rest between freestyle lengths and to
build distance in stages 5–6. Next backstroke step: single-arm (alternating) backstroke, 4× 25 m.

## Cues

- Exhale **all the time** with the face in the water; only inhale when the mouth is out. Holding the breath is
  what makes beginners tired and panicky.
- Look at the bottom of the pool, not forward. Head down = hips and legs up.
- Kick from the hips, legs nearly straight, small and quick.
- Turn the head to breathe, don't lift it.
