## Technical Focus: Sumo Deadlift

### Core Problem
Lift breaks into two phases — full leg extension before back extension. Premature knee lockout is the primary fault.

### Stance Experiments

| Stance | Pro | Con |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Wider + angled feet | Easier off floor | Legs lock out before hips reach |
| Closer + less angled | Smoother lockout | Harder initial pull |

**Current preference:** Closer stance, less angled feet — prioritizes lockout quality over initial pull

### Setup Cues
- "Chest up, push floor" (not "pull bar")
- Shoulders slightly behind bar at start
- Pull slack out before initiating
- Hips and chest rise together

### Supplementary Work

| Exercise | Purpose | Frequency |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Paused Sumo (triple at knee) | Primary technical tool — prevents premature knee lockout | 2×/week |
| Deficit Sumo (1–2 inches) | Forces better positioning off floor | As needed |
| Pin/Block Pulls at knee | Targets sticking point directly | As needed |
| Tempo Sumo (3–5s eccentric) | Teaches hip/torso relationship | As needed |

## Competition Strategy

### Attempt Selection — Corrected Approach

| Attempt | Formula | Example (215 kg training single) |
|---------|---------|----------------------------------|
| 1st | 95–97% of best training single | 215 kg |
| 2nd | +5–7.5% (10–15 kg jump) | 227.5–230 kg |
| 3rd | +2.5–5% from 2nd (only if it moved well) | 235–240 kg |

> **What went wrong in Jan 2026:** Opened at 210 kg (undertrained single was 215 kg) → forced an 8.3% jump to 227.5 kg → no middle ground after a miss.
>
> **Rule:** Never sandbag the opener. Sandbagging forces aggressive jumps that collapse the whole attempt tree.
> **Goal:** Minimum 2/3 successful lifts. 3/3 is the ideal with appropriate selection.

### Platform Psychology

> **Critical insight:** Platform weight feels different than training due to adrenaline dump (crowd, judges, pressure).
> - 227.5 kg felt harder/off-balance, but video showed it was close
> - **Trust training numbers MORE on meet day, not less**
> - Commit to grinding through discomfort — it won't feel like training, that's normal

**Mental preparation:**
- Daily visualization of platform setup
- Practice commitment to difficult lifts
- Build a consistent pre-attempt routine
- Accept that meets require fighting through uncomfortable positions

## Recovery & MRV Warning Signs

> **Reduce volume by 20% if 2 or more of these appear:**
> - Sleep quality decreases
> - Elevated morning heart rate
> - Motivation / enjoyment drops significantly
> - Performance plateaus or regresses
> - Persistent soreness (72+ hours)
> - Technique breaks down under submaximal loads
> - Getting sick frequently
> - Mood / irritability issues

### Fatigue Monitoring
**Primary indicator:** Lower back fatigue (rate 1–10 daily)
- **7+ before a heavy session** → reduce load or volume that session
- Accumulated fatigue requires a deload — do not push through

### Meet Simulation Protocol (1 Week Out)
Full gear: singlet, deadlift slippers, belt, chalk, baby powder
Film from side angle. Assess bar speed, technique, confidence. Use result to finalize attempt selection.

**Deload options:**

| Option | Format | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| A — Active (2 sessions) | S1: 130×3, 150×2, 160×1 / S2: 130×1 ×3 | Maintains feel |
| B — Minimal (1 session) | 130×1, 150×1, 130×1 | Recommended after heavy cycles |

### Non-Negotiables
- **Never progress weight if form breaks down**
- Video all heavy singles
- Check specifically for premature knee lockout in sumo
- Practice perfect form at submaximal loads when fatigued
- Don't sacrifice technique for numbers in training

## Agent Instructions

When advising this athlete:

1. **Squat volume is new** — start conservative (10–12 sets/week); monitor lower back fatigue closely
2. **Sumo lockout is the primary technical fault** — recommend paused sumo and deficit sumo as the main tools
3. **Attempt selection lesson** — opener must be 95–97% of best training single; never sandbag
4. **Face pulls are non-negotiable** — always include them; shoulder health is the priority
5. **Volume progression** — start at lower end; add 2 sets/pattern every 2–3 weeks
6. **Meet prep** — full simulation with gear, filmed, exactly 1 week out
7. **Platform psychology** — weight will feel heavier; trust training numbers more, not less
8. **Recovery markers trump programming** — reduce volume immediately when fatigue signals appear
