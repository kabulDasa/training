# Ring / pinky finger tingling and numbness (ulnar nerve)

Research notes, not a diagnosis. If symptoms persist, get it checked by a doctor or physio (see "When to see
someone" at the bottom).

## Is it the ulnar nerve?

Very likely, if the pattern fits. The **ulnar nerve** supplies feeling to the **pinky and the pinky-side half of
the ring finger** (plus the pinky edge of the hand). Tingling in exactly that area points at the ulnar nerve
somewhere between the neck and the hand. The thumb, index and middle finger are the median nerve (carpal tunnel),
so if those are fine, carpal tunnel is unlikely.

Where it gets squeezed decides the name:

| Site | Name | Clues |
|------|------|-------|
| Elbow (behind the "funny bone", medial epicondyle) | **Cubital tunnel syndrome** (most common) | Worse with the elbow bent for long (sleeping, phone, desk, pull-ups); tapping the funny bone sets off the tingle; tingling also on the **back** of the hand on the pinky side |
| Wrist (Guyon's canal, heel of the hand) | Ulnar tunnel syndrome | Back of the hand feels normal; pressure on the heel of the palm (bar, handlebars, push-up/dip handles, false grip) |
| Neck (C8 nerve root) | Cervical radiculopathy | Neck pain, symptoms change with neck movement, may run into the inner forearm |
| Above the collarbone | Thoracic outlet | Worse with arms overhead, whole arm heavy/tingly |

**Cubital tunnel is the usual one.** Bending the elbow fully stretches the ulnar nerve by several millimetres and
raises the pressure in the tunnel many times over, so anything done with the elbow deeply bent for a long time
irritates it.

### Quick self-checks (pointers only)

- **Elbow flexion test:** bend the elbow fully, wrist straight back, hold 60 s. Tingling in ring/pinky = positive
  for cubital tunnel.
- **Tinel's sign:** tap the groove behind the inner elbow bone. Zing into the pinky = irritated nerve there.
  (Many healthy people are mildly positive; compare both sides.)
- **Back of the hand:** numb on the pinky side of the back of the hand → elbow (or higher). Normal there → more
  likely the wrist.
- **Snapping:** feel the inner elbow while bending and straightening. A nerve that flicks over the bone (ulnar
  subluxation) is common in people with big triceps and makes it worse.

## Likely triggers in this program

- **Pull-ups / pull-up holds / chin-ups:** loaded deep elbow flexion at the top, held. Holds and slow tops are the
  worst for the nerve.
- **Dips:** the bottom position bends the elbow past 90° under load.
- **Tricep extensions** (skull crushers, overhead): full elbow bend with tension on the inside of the elbow.
- **Bicep curls:** the top position, especially with a hard squeeze.
- **False grip / muscle-up work:** pressure on the heel of the hand (Guyon's canal) plus wrist flexion.
- **Squat:** low-bar grip with the elbows high and wrists bent can load the inside elbow.
- **Outside the gym (often the real cause):** sleeping with arms bent under the pillow or head, long phone calls,
  leaning the elbow on a desk / armrest / car door, typing with the elbows sharply bent.

## What to do now (conservative care)

Most mild cases (tingling that comes and goes, no weakness) settle with this in **6–12 weeks**.

1. **Stop leaning on it and stop holding it bent.**
   - At night: keep the elbow fairly straight. Wrap a towel loosely around the elbow (or wear an elbow pad
     backwards, pad over the crease) so it can't bend past ~45°. Night bending is the most common hidden cause.
   - Desk: elbows open past 90°, nothing pressing on the inside of the elbow. Phone on speaker or earbuds.
2. **Change the training, don't just push through it.** See "Training changes" below.
3. **Nerve glides** (below): gentle, a few times a day. They should never cause more than mild tingling that goes
   away within seconds. Evidence that they speed recovery is modest, but they're low risk and help keep the nerve
   moving.
4. **Ice/NSAIDs** won't fix compression. Short-term only if the elbow itself is sore.

### Ulnar nerve glide (sliders, start here)

A "slider" moves the nerve without stretching it (one end tightens while the other loosens):

1. Stand, arm out to the side at shoulder height, palm up, elbow straight.
2. Bend the elbow slowly and bring the palm toward the ear ("waiter / mask" position) **while tilting the head
   toward that shoulder**. Then straighten the elbow while tilting the head away.
3. 10 slow reps, 2–3 × a day. Smooth, no holding.

### Ulnar nerve glide (tensioner, only once sliders are symptom-free)

1. Arm out to the side, palm facing forward and fingers pointing down.
2. Bend the elbow, turn the palm toward you and bring the hand toward the ear, fingers wrapping around like a mask
   over the eye (OK-sign mask).
3. Hold 2–3 s, return. 5–10 reps, once a day. Stop if tingling lingers after you finish.

### Supporting work

- **Wrist / finger:** grip squeezes are fine; avoid hanging for long times in the false grip while symptomatic.
- **Thoracic / scapula:** face pulls (already in the program), band pull-aparts, thoracic extension. Rounded
  shoulders and a forward head add tension along the whole nerve.
- **Neck:** chin tucks, 10 × a day, if the neck seems involved.

## Training changes while symptomatic

| Movement | Change |
|----------|--------|
| Pull-Up / Pull-Up Hold | Cut the hold sets for now. Use a neutral grip if a bar allows it (less ulnar load), keep the top short, drop load to where it's pain/tingle free. Chin-ups (supinated) often bother it more |
| Dip | Shorten to just below parallel (competition depth only, nothing deeper); straps / handles that don't press into the heel of the hand |
| Tricep Extension | Swap skull crushers / overhead for **pushdowns** (elbow never fully bent) |
| Bicep Curl | Stop short of full flexion; hammer curls are usually gentler |
| False grip / muscle-up skill work | Pause until symptoms are gone for 2+ weeks |
| Squat | Wider grip, wrists straight, thumbs-around grip so the elbows don't jam up |

Rule of thumb: **tingling during a set that fades within a minute = OK to keep going at that load. Tingling that
lasts into the evening or the next morning = too much, back off.** Return the full range and load over 2–4 weeks
once you've had a week with no symptoms.

## Prevention

- **Sleep with straighter elbows.** The single biggest everyday lever.
- **Don't rest the inside of the elbow on hard surfaces** (desk, armrest, car window).
- **Program variety for elbow flexion volume:** pull-ups + holds + curls + extensions all bend the elbow fully.
  When volume goes up, watch for early tingle and treat it as a warning sign, like lower-back fatigue 7+/10.
- **Avoid long static holds at the top of pulls** when the elbow is sharply bent; hold at a less bent angle when
  possible.
- **Grip variety:** rotate pronated / neutral / mixed so the same spot isn't loaded every session.
- **Keep the upper back strong and mobile:** face pulls, pull-aparts, thoracic extension.
- **Warm up the elbow** before heavy pull/dip work: a few nerve sliders + light band pushdowns and curls.
- **Elbow sleeves** (allowed in streetlifting) keep the joint warm; they won't stop compression and a too-tight
  sleeve can make it worse. Not a fix.

## When to see someone

Book a doctor / sports physio if any of these:

- **Weakness or clumsiness**: dropping things, trouble pinching (key grip, buttons), weaker grip on the bar, pinky
  that drifts away from the ring finger, ring/pinky starting to "claw".
- **Muscle wasting**: a hollow between the thumb and index finger on the back of the hand, or flattening on the
  pinky side of the palm.
- **Constant numbness** (not just on and off), or symptoms spreading to more fingers / both hands.
- **No better after 6–12 weeks** of the changes above.
- **Neck pain with arm symptoms**, or anything after a fall / impact.

**Go urgently** (same day) for sudden weakness, numbness in both arms or legs, or loss of bladder/bowel control.

A doctor may order **nerve conduction studies / EMG** to confirm the site and severity. Persistent weakness or
muscle wasting is when surgery (decompression or transposition of the nerve) gets discussed. Weakness is a sign
of nerve damage that recovers slowly, so don't wait it out.
